## BRANCH 1: COMPUTING HISTORY

**Computing History** — The technological and institutional development of computers from prehistory to the present

### 1.1 Three Traditions of Automation (Prehistory)
- **Administration tradition** — Census, business, insurance: Hollerith tabulating machines (1890 US Census)
- **Process control tradition** — Engineering/infrastructure: Dutch waterworks, Taylor's scientific management
- **Science tradition** — Physics, astronomy, military ballistics: equations too complex for human calculation

### 1.2 Early Machines
- **Babbage Difference Engine (1822)** — First mechanical calculator design; never completed
- **Babbage Analytical Engine (1837)** — First concept of a programmable machine; Lovelace wrote first algorithm
- **Hollerith Tabulating Machine (1890)** — Punched-card system for US Census; founded IBM predecessor
- **Harvard Mark I (1944)** — Aiken's electromechanical computer; IBM collaboration; Babbage narrative used for funding

### 1.3 WWII and the First Electronic Computers
- **ENIAC (1945)** — Moore School (Mauchly + Eckert + Goldstine); first electronic general-purpose computer; funded by BRL for ballistics
- **EDVAC + First Draft (1945)** — Von Neumann; stored-program concept; controversial authorship
- **Moore School Lectures (1946)** — Key knowledge-transfer event; spread stored-program design to UK and Europe
- **EDSAC (1949)** — Maurice Wilkes, Cambridge; first practical stored-program computer; first programming manual (Wilkes/Wheeler/Gill 1951)
- **Manchester Baby (1948)** — Williams + Newman; first stored-program run; UK parallel to EDSAC
- **Atanasoff-Berry Computer (ABC, 1942)** — Early electronic computer; passed over in historical narrative

### 1.4 Dutch Dinosaurs
- **H.A. Lorentz & C. Lely** — Waterworks calculations 1910s–20s; administrative/process control tradition
- **Johan van Veen (1893–1953)** — Rijkswaterstaat; *Deltar* analogue computer for flood control
- **Aad van Wijngaarden (1916–1987)** — Mathematisch Centrum Amsterdam (1947); ARRA (June 21, 1952); ALGOL 60 co-creator
- **ARRA (1952)** — First Dutch electronic computer; played Dutch national anthem (sounds)
- **ARRA II (1954)** — Transistors + speaker; successor to ARRA

### 1.5 Cold War Computing
- **Manhattan Project** — Nuclear weapons computation; drove need for electronic computers
- **SAGE (1958–63)** — Cold War air-defence; 23 direction centers; IBM AN/FSQ-7; obsolete by ICBMs but huge tech spinoff
- **Project Whirlwind (1944–53)** — MIT; Jay Forrester; Perry Crawford (digital conversion); Bill Papian (core memory 1951); became SAGE
- **Soviet computing** — БЭСМ-6 (1963); parallel development ignored in Western narrative
- **Polish mathematicians** — Rejewski + Różycki + Zygalski broke Enigma (1932); precede Turing; absent from standard history

### 1.6 Computer Industry Development
- **IBM Model 650 (1954)** — First mass-produced computer; dominated market
- **IBM System/360 (1964)** — Single compatible family; $5bn gamble; huge success; software crisis driver
- **IBM and the seven dwarfs** — IBM ~75% market; Sperry Rand, Honeywell, RCA, GE, Control Data, Burroughs, NCR
- **IBM and the BUNCH** — After 1971 recession; RCA and GE exit; Burroughs, UNIVAC, NCR, Control Data, Honeywell remain
- **Eckert & Mauchly / UNIVAC** — EMCC startup; UNIVAC for Census Bureau; 1952 election prediction on CBS
- **IBM 1401 (1959)** — Cheap mainframe; IBM 1403 fast printer; RPG; 12,000 sold

### 1.7 Software Era
- **IBM Unbundling (1968)** — Software priced separately from hardware; created software industry
- **Software Crisis (1968)** — NATO conference; OS/360 as exemplar; Dijkstra's response
- **ALGOL 60** — European academic language; van Wijngaarden + Rutishauser + Bauer + Backus; elegant but commercially rejected
- **FORTRAN (1957)** — IBM; practical for scientists; American commercial agenda
- **COBOL (1959)** — Grace Hopper; business language; natural language vision; committee-designed
- **LISP (1958)** — McCarthy; lambda calculus (Church 1936); AI programming language

### 1.8 Timesharing and Personal Computing
- **CTSS (1961)** — MIT; first timesharing system
- **BASIC (1963)** — Kemeny & Kurtz, Dartmouth; first accessible programming language for non-specialists
- **Whole Earth Catalogue (1968)** — Stewart Brand; counterculture + technology; computing as liberation
- **Homebrew Computer Club (1975)** — Silicon Valley hacker culture; Apple origin story
- **Apple II (1977) / IBM PC (1981)** — Personal computer mass market
- **Apple Macintosh (1984)** — GUI; mouse; desktop metaphor; "computer for the rest of us"

### 1.9 Internet Era
- **ARPANET (1969)** — Military/DARPA; packet switching; survivable network
- **Email (1971)** — Tomlinson; unintended appropriation; became dominant ARPANET use
- **World Wide Web (1989)** — Berners-Lee; CERN; designed for physics paper sharing
- **Mosaic browser (1993)** — Made web visually accessible; triggered commercialisation
- **Google (1998)** — PageRank; surveillance capitalism; AdWords (2000)
- **Web 2.0 (2004+)** — User-generated content; social networking; data as product
- **Netflix** — Streaming + recommendation engine; surveillance capitalism without ads (originally)
- **GDPR (2018)** — EU data protection; fundamental rights approach; right to erasure

## BRANCH 2: AI HISTORY

**AI History** — The development of ideas and systems aimed at replicating or extending human intelligence

### 2.1 Intellectual Prehistory (Pre-1940)
- **Aristotle's syllogism (350 BCE)** — Formal logic as foundation of reasoning; first systematic attempt
- **Ramon Llull Ars Magna (13th c.)** — Mechanical combination of concepts; proto-AI dream
- **Leibniz Calculus Ratiocinator (17th c.)** — Universal logical calculus; formal language dream
- **Boole Boolean algebra (1854)** — Logic as mathematics; foundation of digital computing
- **Frege Begriffsschrift (1879)** — Formal logic notation; Principia inspiration
- **Russell & Whitehead Principia Mathematica (1910–13)** — All mathematics from logical axioms; automated reasoning dream
- **Esperanto (1887) / Artificial languages** — Universal language projects; relevance: formal unambiguous communication = AI dream

### 2.2 Foundations (1930s–1950s)
- **Church Lambda Calculus (1936)** — Mathematical foundation for computation; → LISP
- **Gödel Incompleteness (1931)** — Limits of formal systems; computability limits
- **Turing Computability (1936)** — Turing machine; halting problem; theoretical limits of computation
- **Cybernetics (Wiener, 1948)** — Information + control + feedback; machine = animal; defining technology of 1960s
- **Turing Test (1950)** — "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"; intelligence as imitation; Alan Turing
- **Dartmouth Conference (1956)** — McCarthy + Minsky + Shannon + others; "Artificial Intelligence" coined; field founded

### 2.3 Classical AI Era (1956–1970s)
- **LISP (McCarthy, 1958)** — Symbolic AI language; list processing; lambda calculus basis
- **General Problem Solver (Newell & Simon, 1957)** — Universal problem-solving algorithm; early AI system
- **LOGO (Papert, MIT, 1967)** — AI + education; constructivist learning; turtle graphics
- **Expert systems (1970s–80s)** — MYCIN, DENDRAL; knowledge-based AI; dominated 1980s
- **Nim game (Strachey, 1951)** — Ferranti Mark I; first computer game; appropriation of computing for AI demo
- **Lighthill Report (1973)** — UK evaluation; concluded AI failed to deliver; → first AI winter

### 2.4 AI Winters and Resurgences
- **First AI winter (1970s)** — Lighthill Report; funding cuts; overpromising on machine translation
- **Expert systems boom (1980s)** — LISP machines; knowledge engineering; Japan 5th Generation project
- **Second AI winter (late 1980s–90s)** — Expert systems brittle; LISP machines commercial failure
- **Statistical/ML turn (1990s)** — Support vector machines; statistical NLP; less "AI", more "machine learning"
- **Deep learning revolution (2012)** — Hinton AlexNet; ImageNet; GPU computing; → current AI summer
- **Large Language Models (2017+)** — Transformer architecture; GPT; BERT; generative AI

### 2.5 AI and Science
- **Mathematics and AI** — Principia → formal proof → Church → Turing → LISP → theorem proving
- **MacKenzie Mechanizing Proof** — Proof checkers in military systems; Patriot missile
- **Perceptron (Rosenblatt, 1957)** — First neural network; Minsky/Papert critique (1969) → first neural net winter
- **Backpropagation (Rumelhart, 1986)** — Neural network training algorithm; second wave

### 2.6 AI and Education
- **Programmed Instruction (Skinner)** — Behaviourist; stimulus-response; computer as teaching machine
- **LOGO (Papert, 1967)** — Constructivist; child programs computer; turtle graphics; discovery learning
- **MOOCs (2012+)** — Massive open online courses; digital education at scale
- **AI tutors (2020s)** — ChatGPT in education; debates continue Skinner vs. Papert

## BRANCH 3: KEY CONCEPTS

**Key Concepts** — Analytical frameworks for understanding computing and AI history

### 3.1 Appropriation
- **Definition** — Using technology for purposes its designers did not intend
- **Nim (1951)** — Scientific computer → game → AI demonstration
- **Email (1971)** — ARPANET communication tool → dominant social application
- **GPU** — Games graphics processor → deep learning infrastructure (AlexNet 2012)
- **SAGE → SABRE** — Military air defence → civilian airline reservations
- **Internet → Social media** — Academic/military → commercial/personal

### 3.2 Agendas
- **Definition** — Interests and goals that shape how historical narratives are constructed
- **Aiken invokes Babbage** — Uses predecessor narrative to secure Harvard/IBM funding
- **AI winter narrative** — Used to argue for sustained investment OR to warn against hype
- **McCorduck Machines Who Think** — AI advocacy through historical narrative
- **Anglo-American narrative** — Ignores Polish, Dutch, Soviet contributions
- **FORTRAN vs ALGOL agendas** — Commercial pragmatism vs. academic idealism

### 3.3 Defining Technology (Bolter)
- **Definition** — Technology that reshapes cultural self-understanding; provides metaphors for human nature
- **Clock (17th c.)** — Humans as clockwork machines
- **Steam engine (19th c.)** — Society as energy system
- **Cybernetics (1960s)** — Brain as information processor; mind as feedback system
- **AI (21st c.)** — Intelligence as computation; creativity as pattern recognition
- **Mechanism** — Technology provides vocabulary that spreads beyond technical domain

### 3.4 Surveillance Capitalism
- **Definition (Zuboff)** — Human behaviour as raw material → prediction products → sold to advertisers
- **Google AdWords (2000)** — Search data → targeted advertising → surveillance capitalism birth
- **Netflix algorithm** — Watch behaviour → prediction → content commissioning → engagement maximisation
- **GDPR (2018)** — EU response; data as fundamental right; explicit consent
- **Section 230 (US)** — Platform immunity; market self-regulation approach

### 3.5 AI Winters
- **First winter (1970s)** — Lighthill Report; machine translation failure; funding cuts
- **Second winter (late 1980s)** — Expert systems collapse; LISP machines commercial failure
- **Narrative use** — Winter → spring framing used to justify sustained investment
- **Babbage parallel** — Over-promising → abandonment → revival narrative
- **Rhetorical function** — Both warning (critics) and motivation (advocates)

### 3.6 Digital Culture
- **Definition** — Transformation of existing practices by digital technology's specific logic (data, personalisation, platforms)
- **Music** — Vinyl → CD → streaming; access expanded; artist revenue compressed
- **Journalism** — Print gatekeeping → algorithmic virality; democratisation + misinformation
- **Banking** — Manual → ATM → algorithmic credit scoring
- **Education** — Teacher → teaching machine → LOGO → MOOC → AI tutor
- **Cultural ambivalence** — Computers as liberation AND surveillance; 1980s onward

### 3.7 Technology and Trust
- **Definition** — Technical capability alone insufficient for adoption; social trust must be constructed
- **Hollerith (1890)** — Census machine; trust built through speed + legible output
- **UNIVAC CBS (1952)** — Election prediction as public demonstration
- **IBM brand** — "Nobody ever got fired for buying IBM"; institutional trust as risk reduction
- **Atanasoff (1940s)** — Technically functional but no institutional trust network → passed over

## BRANCH 4: KEY FIGURES

**Key Figures** — People central to the histories of computing and AI

### 4.1 Computing Pioneers
- **Charles Babbage (1791–1871)** — Difference Engine + Analytical Engine; never completed; computing precursor
- **Ada Lovelace (1815–1852)** — First programmer; notes on Analytical Engine; algorithm concept
- **Herman Hollerith (1860–1929)** — Tabulating machine; 1890 US Census; founded IBM predecessor (CTR)
- **Howard Aiken (1900–1973)** — Harvard Mark I; used Babbage narrative for funding; IBM collaboration
- **John Mauchly (1907–1980)** — Moore School; co-inventor of ENIAC with Eckert
- **J. Presper Eckert (1919–1995)** — Moore School; co-inventor of ENIAC; UNIVAC with Mauchly
- **John von Neumann (1903–1957)** — EDVAC First Draft; stored-program concept; mathematical genius
- **Maurice Wilkes (1913–2010)** — EDSAC; first programming manual; Moore School Lectures attendee
- **Grace Hopper (1906–1992)** — First compiler; COBOL; "bug" populariser; naval officer; women in computing icon
- **Edsger Dijkstra (1930–2002)** — Structured programming; software crisis response; Dutch; "GOTO harmful"

### 4.2 AI Pioneers
- **Alan Turing (1912–1954)** — Computability (1936); Turing Test (1950); Enigma codebreaking; "father of AI" (contested)
- **John McCarthy (1927–2011)** — Dartmouth 1956; "Artificial Intelligence" coined; LISP (1958)
- **Norbert Wiener (1894–1964)** — Cybernetics (1948); information theory + control theory; defining technology of 1960s
- **Marvin Minsky (1927–2016)** — Dartmouth; perceptron critique (with Papert 1969); MIT AI Lab
- **Claude Shannon (1916–2001)** — Information theory; chess-playing computer (1950); Dartmouth
- **Seymour Papert (1928–2016)** — LOGO; constructivist education; MIT; turtle graphics
- **Arthur Samuel (1901–1990)** — Checkers-learning programme (1959); first machine learning demonstration

### 4.3 European/Dutch Figures
- **Aad van Wijngaarden (1916–1987)** — Mathematisch Centrum; ARRA; ALGOL 60; Dutch computing founder
- **Johan van Veen (1893–1953)** — Rijkswaterstaat; Deltar; Dutch process control tradition
- **Marian Rejewski (1905–1980)** — Polish mathematician; broke Enigma (1932); absent from standard history
- **Jerzy Różycki (1909–1942)** — Polish mathematician; Enigma codebreaker; died at sea
- **Henryk Zygalski (1908–1978)** — Polish mathematician; Enigma codebreaker; "perforated sheets" method
- **Stafford Beer (1926–2002)** — Cybernetics in management; Project Cybersyn (Chile 1971–73)

### 4.4 Silicon Valley / Industry
- **Thomas Watson Sr. (1874–1956)** — IBM founder; sceptical of computers; "rational but wrong"
- **Thomas Watson Jr. (1914–1993)** — IBM CEO; bet on System/360; succeeded Watson Sr.
- **Jay Forrester (b.1918)** — Whirlwind / SAGE; core memory; real-time computing
- **Perry Crawford** — ONR; converted Whirlwind to digital; also involved in SABRE
- **Sergey Brin & Larry Page (b.1973/1973)** — Google; PageRank; surveillance capitalism
- **Steve Jobs (1955–2011) & Steve Wozniak (b.1950)** — Apple; personal computer; Macintosh; Silicon Valley myth
- **Stewart Brand (b.1938)** — Whole Earth Catalogue; counterculture + computing; "access to tools"

### 4.5 Historians and Theorists
- **J. David Bolter** — *Turing's Man* (1984); defining technology concept
- **Shoshana Zuboff** — *Surveillance Capitalism* (2019); behavioural data as raw material
- **Pamela McCorduck** — *Machines Who Think*; AI advocacy history; no-bonus question context
- **Eden Medina** — *Cybernetic Revolutionaries*; Project Cybersyn; socialist cybernetics
- **Stephanie Dick** — AI history + cultural dispositions; Marxist computing history
- **Jonnie Penn** — "Animo Nullius"; data as *terra nullius*; colonial framing of AI data
- **Martin Campbell-Kelly** — *Computer: A History of the Information Machine*; economic/social lens; B-question source
