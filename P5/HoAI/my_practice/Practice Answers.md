### **AI and education**: To what extent was the 1950s/60s hype of programmed instruction instrumental in the visibility of AI? Make a comparison to the 1970s/80s hype of LOGO

Thesis: Both programmed instruction and LOGO made AI visible to the public and to funders — but in opposite ways. Programmed instruction sold AI as a tool of
  institutional efficiency; LOGO sold it as a tool of individual liberation.

  ---
  Body 1 — Programmed Instruction (Skinner, 1950s–60s)
  - B.F. Skinner's behaviourist model: break knowledge into steps, immediate feedback, computers as teaching machines
  - Made AI visible by giving it a mass application — education was something everyone understood and cared about
  - Framed AI as useful, scalable, institutional → attracted government + military funding
  - BUT: critics said it was mechanical, dehumanising, not real intelligence — just stimulus-response
  - Visibility came at a cost: it defined AI narrowly as rule-following automation

  Body 2 — LOGO (Papert, MIT, 1967)
  - Constructivist model: children learn by programming a turtle, discovering maths through play
  - Made AI visible differently — as a creative, humanistic tool, not a replacement for teachers
  - Huge hype in 1970s–80s: schools bought computers specifically for LOGO; it was on the cover of magazines
  - Connected AI to the personal computer revolution (same era as Apple II, BBC Micro)
  - BUT: the hype outran the evidence — learning outcomes were disappointing; faded by late 1980s

  Body 3 — The comparison / what it means
  - Both were hype cycles that made AI visible beyond university labs — into schools, homes, public discourse
  - Both followed the AI winters pattern: big promises → mainstream attention → disappointment
  - Key difference: programmed instruction served an institutional agenda (efficiency, control); LOGO served a countercultural agenda (liberation, creativity) —
  same technology, opposite politics
  - Both show that AI visibility has always depended on attaching AI to something people already care about (education, children)
