# COMP 2711 - Discrete Mathematical Tools for CS

## Logistics

- **Course:** COMP 2711, Discrete Mathematical Tools for Computer Science, HKUST (formerly COMP 170)
- **Textbook:** *Discrete Math for Computer Science* by Bogart, Stein, Drysdale (slides by M. J. Golin and G. Trippen)
- No instructor/TA/schedule info in the saved slides; check Canvas for the current offering.

## Topics (lecture decks on hand)

1. Logic: propositions, truth tables, equivalences, implication
2. Quantifiers and predicate logic, negation rules
3. Inference: proof methods (direct, contraposition, contradiction), 12 rules of inference
4. Modular arithmetic + intro cryptography (Caesar cipher, $Z_n$)
5. Inverses and GCDs: Euclidean + extended Euclidean algorithm, Bezout
6. RSA: Fermat's little theorem, key generation, correctness proof, fast exponentiation, CRT
7. Induction: smallest counterexample, weak/strong induction
8. Advanced induction: Big-O proofs of recurrences, strengthening the hypothesis
9. Recursion and recurrences: first-order linear, Theorem 4.1/4.5
10. Solving recurrences: iteration/expansion, divide-and-conquer, three-behavior lemma
11. Basic counting: sum/product/bijection/division rules
12. More counting: lists, functions, permutations, combinations
13. Binomial coefficients: Pascal, binomial theorem, combinatorial proofs
14. Inclusion-exclusion: PIE, derangements, surjections
15. Probability: sample spaces, axioms, uniform distributions
16. Conditional probability and independence
17. Random variables, expectation, binomial/geometric distributions
18. Variance, standard deviation, Central Limit Theorem
19. Intro graphs: degrees, connectivity, trees

## Exam structure (from past exams F2023-F2025, S2023-S2025)

**Important: Fall and Spring offerings split topics differently.**

- **Fall midterms**: logic, quantifiers, inference, sets/countability, counting, combinatorial proofs, inclusion-exclusion, pigeonhole (F2025 also had ~23 pts probability). NO number theory or induction.
- **Spring midterms**: logic, quantifiers, contradiction proofs, countability, induction (Peano/structural), number theory (Fermat, extended Euclid, CRT, RSA), some asymptotics. NO counting or probability.
- **Finals**: cumulative, ~25-35% pre-midterm + 65-75% post-midterm. Reliable topics: counting, Bayes/conditional probability, linearity of expectation with indicators, recurrences (expansion + induction proof), CRT, Fermat, RSA, big-O classification, graphs.

## Exam format and rules (recent years)

- 100 points, 9-12 written questions, 2-3 hours. No multiple choice (some rapid-fire T/F subparts need no justification).
- **Cheat sheet:** midterm 1 A4; final 1-2 A4 (varies by year; F2024/F2025 handwritten or printed, double-sided).
- Answers may be left as $\binom{n}{k}$, $P(n,k)$, factorials; nonstandard nCk notation disallowed; F2025 banned summation notation in counting answers and required pen.
- Many parts force a method: "no truth tables", "must use inclusion-exclusion", "must use extended Euclidean algorithm showing steps", "master theorem not allowed". Answer-only responses on CRT-type questions score 0.
- All recent past-exam PDFs include official solutions (S2024 has grading rubrics).
