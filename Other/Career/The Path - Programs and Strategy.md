# The Path - Programs and Strategy

How @hinumpy went from zero connections to internships and fellowships all four years of college: the exact programs plus the strategy behind it. Last updated July 2026. Note: US-centric, but the strategy in Part 2 transfers anywhere.

## Part 1: The Programs That Opened Doors

Structured, application-based programs. Free, no existing connections required, look strong on a resume. Apply broadly, most people don't, which is your advantage.

### Break Through Tech AI (Cornell Tech)
breakthroughtech.org

A free, year-long applied AI program run out of Cornell Tech, with hubs at partner universities, that pairs you with real companies for a hands-on ML apprenticeship.

- Who it's for: undergrad women and underrepresented students in CS or related majors, no prior ML experience required
- Timing: runs late May through April the following year. Applications open in January for the next cohort
- Do this now: fill out the interest form so you get notified the second applications open

### Google Summer of Code (GSoC)
summerofcode.withgoogle.com

A remote, stipend-paid open source fellowship. You get matched with a mentor organization and ship real code to a live project over the summer.

- Who it's for: 18+, new or beginner open source contributors (under 2 years experience). You do not have to be a current student
- Timing: 2026 contributor applications ran March 16 to 31, results April 30. Expect a similar window every year
- Do this now: pick 2 to 3 organizations from the GSoC org list in January or February and start making small contributions (docs, small bugs) before applications open. This is what makes your application stand out

### NASA L'Space Academy (ASU)
lspace.asu.edu

A free, fully online program where you work on a real mission concept team (Mission Concept Academy) or write a mock proposal (NPWEE).

- Who it's for: undergrads enrolled at a US college or university (grad students can apply to NPWEE). Needs a laptop, webcam, about 6 to 10 hours a week
- Timing: applications open around fall for spring cohorts, cycles repeat through the year
- Do this now: apply as soon as a cycle opens. Rolling and free, no reason to wait

Program timelines shift year to year. Always confirm current dates on the official sites before planning around them.

## Part 2: What To Do With Zero Connections

Programs alone don't get you hired. None of this requires knowing someone. It requires showing up consistently.

1. **Get a leadership role in a tech club, not just a member.** Leadership roles give you real projects to talk about and put you in the room with professors and guest speakers.
2. **Take an on-campus job in your field.** Not glamorous, but real technical experience, a paycheck, and another line proving you show up and solve problems.
3. **Get certified.** Certifications are one of the few things you can fully control. No application, no interview, just study and pass.
4. **Go to hackathons and build your own projects.** Hackathons force you to ship something in 24 to 48 hours with strangers. Go even when you feel underqualified. This is where you meet your people.
5. **Don't sleep on career fairs and networking events.** Don't go just to drop a resume, go to actually talk to recruiters and ask questions. Go consistently, not just once.
6. **Build real relationships with professors.** Office hours, follow-up emails, asking for advice, not just grades. Professors write recommendation letters and know about opportunities before they're posted.
7. **Find your people, especially in a male-dominated field.** Friendships with other women in tech make the hard parts (imposter syndrome, all-nighters, rejections) far more survivable.
8. **Share what you learn while you're learning it.** Teaching what you just learned cements it, and gets you noticed.

Do this now: pick one of these eight and do it this month. Not all eight, just one.
