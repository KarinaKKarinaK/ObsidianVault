# History of Robotics

Tags: #robotics #history

---

## Ancient & Pre-Modern

- **~400 BCE**: Archytas of Tarentum builds a mechanical pigeon powered by steam — one of the earliest known self-propelled mechanical devices.
- **1495**: Leonardo da Vinci designs a mechanical knight capable of sitting up, waving arms, and moving its head.
- **1738**: Jacques de Vaucanson builds the "Digesting Duck" — a mechanical automaton mimicking a duck eating and digesting food.

---

## Early Modern (1900s–1950s)

- **1921**: Czech playwright Karel Čapek coins the word **"robot"** in his play *R.U.R. (Rossum's Universal Robots)*. Derived from Czech *robota* = forced labor.
- **1942**: Isaac Asimov formulates the **Three Laws of Robotics** in *Runaround*:
  1. A robot may not injure a human being.
  2. A robot must obey orders given by humans (unless it conflicts with Law 1).
  3. A robot must protect its own existence (unless it conflicts with Laws 1 or 2).
- **1948–1949**: William Grey Walter builds the "tortoises" (Elmer and Elsie) — early autonomous mobile robots with light and touch sensors.
- **1950**: Alan Turing publishes "Computing Machinery and Intelligence" — philosophical groundwork for AI.
- **1954**: **George Devol** patents the first digitally operated and programmable robot: the **Unimate**.

---

## Industrial Era (1960s–1980s)

- **1961**: **Unimate** deployed at a General Motors plant in New Jersey — the first industrial robot on an assembly line.
- **1966**: Stanford Research Institute begins work on **Shakey** — first mobile robot to use AI for reasoning and planning. Completed ~1972.
- **1969**: Victor Scheinman develops the **Stanford Arm** — first electrically powered, computer-controlled robot arm.
- **1970**: WABOT-1 project begins at Waseda University — first full-scale anthropomorphic humanoid robot.
- **1973**: Ichiro Kato (Waseda) completes **WABOT-1** — can measure/adapt to environment, grip objects, walk.
- **1979**: Stanford Cart navigates a chair-filled room autonomously — early autonomous vehicle milestone.
- **1981**: Takeo Kanade et al. develop the **Direct Drive Arm** at CMU — higher speed and precision through motor-joint coupling.
- **1986**: Honda begins its humanoid robotics program.
- **1988**: Mark Raibert's **Leg Laboratory** at CMU produces one-legged hopping robots, then biped, then quadruped — foundational work for legged locomotion.

---

## Modern Robotics (1990s–2000s)

- **1996**: Honda unveils P2 humanoid prototype.
- **2000**: **ASIMO** (Advanced Step in Innovative Mobility) — Honda's flagship humanoid. Can walk, run, climb stairs.
- **1997**: NASA's **Sojourner** rover lands on Mars — first robot to operate on another planet.
- **1997**: ROS precursors emerge. Andrew Ng's STAIR project at Stanford is a key driver.
- **2001**: Cynthia Breazeal's **Kismet** at MIT — first social robot capable of recognizing and simulating human emotions.
- **2002**: **iRobot** (co-founded by Rodney Brooks) releases the **Roomba** — most widely deployed consumer robot in history.
- **2004**: DARPA Grand Challenge — first autonomous vehicle race (no finisher 2004; Stanley from Stanford wins 2005).
- **2005**: **BigDog** by Boston Dynamics — quadruped robot traversing rough terrain. DARPA-funded.
- **2007**: **ROS** (Robot Operating System) released by Willow Garage — becomes dominant open-source robotics middleware.

---

## Deep Learning Era (2010s)

- **2012**: AlexNet wins ImageNet — triggers deep learning revolution; transforms computer vision for robots.
- **2013**: **DARPA Robotics Challenge (DRC)** announced — pushes disaster-response humanoid robots. Atlas by Boston Dynamics competes.
- **2015**: Boston Dynamics releases Atlas doing parkour.
- **2016**: AlphaGo (DeepMind) defeats Lee Sedol — demonstrates deep RL at superhuman level; influences robotics RL research.
- **2016**: Pieter Abbeel, John Schulman (OpenAI) publish **TRPO** and later **PPO** — fundamental RL algorithms widely used for robot training.
- **2017**: OpenAI's **Dactyl** project begins — Shadow Dexterous Hand solving Rubik's Cube using RL + domain randomization.
- **2018**: **Spot** by Boston Dynamics released commercially — quadruped for enterprise.
- **2019**: OpenAI **Dactyl** solves Rubik's Cube one-handed — major dexterous manipulation milestone.
- **2019**: Boston Dynamics Atlas performs gymnastics routine.

---

## Foundation Model Era (2020s)

- **2021**: NVIDIA releases **Isaac Gym** — GPU-accelerated RL training for robots in simulation; massive speedup.
- **2022**: Google Research publishes **SayCan** — LLM grounded in robot affordances to reason about tasks.
- **2022**: **RT-1 (Robotics Transformer 1)** — transformer trained on 130k robot demonstrations, multi-task manipulation.
- **2023**: **RT-2** by Google DeepMind — VLA model built on PaLI-X, establishes the vision-language-action paradigm.
- **2023**: **Open X-Embodiment** dataset — 1M+ trajectories from 22 robot types, 34 labs. Enables cross-embodiment training.
- **2023**: **ACT** (Action Chunking with Transformers) — Tony Zhao (Stanford/MIT); fine-grained bimanual manipulation from ~10 min of demos.
- **2023**: **Diffusion Policy** (Chi et al., Columbia) — uses diffusion models as robot action policy.
- **2024**: **π0 (pi-zero)** by Physical Intelligence — first large-scale generalist VLA using flow matching.
- **2024**: Boston Dynamics releases **electric Atlas** — retires hydraulic version; commercial deployment begins.
- **2025**: **Gemini Robotics** (Google DeepMind) — VLA model built on Gemini 2.0; supports humanoids and diverse robot forms.
- **2025**: Figure AI's **BotQ factory** — first purpose-built humanoid factory; targeting 12,000 units/year.
