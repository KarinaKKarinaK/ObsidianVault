# HAI — Last 30 Min Cheat Sheet
> If you know nothing else, know this. Fast connections, big ideas, killer sentences.

---

## HOW BECKERS LIKES ANSWERS WRITTEN

He is a **historian of science** who thinks in **stories and narratives**. He does not want a list of facts. He wants you to:

1. **Tell a story with a twist.** Not "IBM succeeded because of good technology" but "IBM was late, sceptical, and almost missed the computer entirely — yet ended up dominating the industry. The twist is that rational business logic led them astray."
2. **Name the villain or the irony.** Every good story has tension. SAGE was built for safety but offered none. UNIVAC was brilliant but its owners didn't know what to do with it. LOGO promised a revolution in education and quietly faded.
3. **Show that nothing was inevitable.** The best sentence you can write: *"This almost didn't happen."* Whirlwind almost got cancelled. IBM almost stayed with punch cards. The internet almost stayed military. Contingency = historical thinking.
4. **End with an open question.** Beckers loves answers that don't fully close — that gesture toward *"and this tension is still unresolved today."* It shows you think like a historian, not a student reciting facts.
5. **Make the analytical move explicit.** Don't just use "agendas" — say the word. Don't just describe appropriation — say "this is appropriation in action." Naming the concept signals you understood the course.

## KILLER SENTENCE STARTERS

**To open (thesis):**
- "X is best understood not as [obvious reading] but as [your twist]."
- "The history of X is, at its core, a history of [agendas / appropriation / trust / competing desires]."
- "To a large extent, X — but with one crucial qualification: [complication]."

**To introduce an example:**
- "The clearest illustration of this is…"
- "This almost didn't happen: …"
- "The irony is that…"
- "What makes this case significant is not [the obvious thing] but [the mechanism]."

**To add a counterpoint:**
- "One might object that… This is true — but it does not undo the argument, because…"
- "Conversely, [X] shows the limits of this claim."
- "The unintended-vs-deliberate distinction matters here:…"

**To connect A to B (C-questions):**
- "What connects these two cases is not [surface similarity] but [deeper logic]."
- "Both follow the same pattern: [name the pattern]."
- "The relation is one of [causation / tension / analogy] — and that distinction matters because…"

**To conclude:**
- "In sum, [restate thesis in new words]."
- "The deeper point is that [technology / narrative] is never neutral — it serves [who] by doing [what]."
- "Whether this tension will be resolved in the era of [LLMs / AI regulation / platform monopolies] remains the open question."

**The historiographical sentence (use in every answer):**
- "This telling serves [X] agenda — recognising that does not invalidate the history, but asks us to read it critically."

## QUICK CONNECTIONS — WHEN YOU SEE THIS TOPIC, TELL THESE STORIES

### "Scientific machines → business / computers becoming commercial"
Tell 2–3 of:
- **Watson rational but wrong** — IBM almost missed the computer because rational business logic said demand was tiny; Eckert & Mauchly were irrational but right; UNIVAC on CBS 1952 = trust via spectacle
- **IBM 1401** — RPG let non-programmers generate reports; leasing model removed financial risk; "data processing" framing made it familiar; 12,000 sold vs. 1,000 forecast
- **Moore School "means to an end"** — ENIAC was built for ballistics, not computing; the computer had no identity of its own yet
- **Appropriation of SAGE → SABRE** — military real-time computing becomes airline reservations; technology finds its commercial life through unintended use

---

### "Data & AI / surveillance capitalism / Web 2.0"
Tell 2–3 of:
- **Google PageRank → AdWords** — better search (1998) + revenue problem solved by targeting (2000) = user behaviour as raw material; Zuboff: "prediction products"
- **Netflix** — every pause, every re-watch = data; House of Cards commissioned algorithmically; engagement maximisation = behaviour modification; user pays with money AND data
- **ImageNet → AlexNet** — web-scraped images (Web 2.0 data) train the neural network (2012) that starts the deep learning revolution; Web 2.0 data IS AI training data
- **GDPR vs. Section 230** — EU: data as fundamental right, explicit consent, fines; US: self-regulation, free speech, platforms not liable; two legal philosophies, same underlying tension

---

### "AI in Education"
Tell 2–3 of:
- **Skinner's programmed instruction** — behaviourist, top-down, computers as teaching machines; gave AI a mass application and government funding; but defined AI narrowly as rule-following
- **Papert's LOGO (MIT, 1967)** — constructivist (Piaget), bottom-up, turtle graphics; AI as creative tool; huge 1980s hype on Apple II + BBC Micro; faded when outcomes disappointed
- **The contrast** — same technology, opposite politics: institutional control (Skinner) vs. countercultural liberation (Papert); both are hype cycles that mirror AI winters
- **AI effect** — every time AI achieves something in education, it stops being called "real" AI; the goalposts move; this is how the field defines itself

---

### "Histories / agendas / alternative starting points"
Tell 2–3 of:
- **Aiken → Babbage** — Aiken invokes Babbage's unfinished Analytical Engine to get Harvard/IBM funding for Mark I; the machines were architecturally different; history as funding tool
- **McCorduck** — writes *Machines Who Think* as an AI advocate; the AI winter/spring narrative argues for sustained investment; history legitimises the field
- **Dartmouth 1956 as origin** — McCarthy chose this as AI's founding moment; it serves an American, symbolic-AI, male-dominated agenda; alternative origins (Turing 1950, Wiener 1948, Strachey/Nim 1951) tell different stories
- **Polish codebreakers** — Rejewski, Różycki, Zygalski broke Enigma before Turing (from 1932); excluded from the British/Turing narrative; prejudice in historiography

---

### "Appropriation"
Tell 2–3 of:
- **Ferranti Mark I → Nim (1951)** — scientific computer, Strachey plays a game; first AI demonstration; appropriation births the field
- **SAGE → SABRE** — military air defence → airline reservations → credit cards → ATMs; real-time computing's value came entirely from unintended civilian use
- **GPU → AlexNet (2012)** — video game chip appropriated by Hinton's team; triggers deep learning; without games hardware, no modern AI
- **WWW → e-commerce** — Berners-Lee designed it for physics paper sharing at CERN (1989); appropriated for Amazon (1994), Facebook (2004), YouTube (2005)

---

### "Defining technology (Bolter)"
Tell 2–3 of:
- **Cybernetics (Wiener, 1948)** — brain as feedback system; reshapes psychology (cognitive science), management (Stafford Beer, Project Cybersyn Chile 1971), philosophy; AI gets its vocabulary from cybernetics
- **Generative AI (2020s)** — forces redefinition of creativity, authorship, originality; "hallucination" and "prompt engineering" enter everyday language; law, education, art all restructuring
- **AlphaFold (DeepMind, 2020)** — solves protein folding; reshapes what biological knowledge looks like and who can produce it; computation redefines science
- **The complication** — Bolter requires *durable* reshaping; AI's status isn't settled yet; we may be in a hype cycle before another winter; flag this as a qualification

---

### "Trust"
Tell 2–3 of:
- **Hollerith 1890** — census machine; officials had to trust a machine with national data; succeeded by making output legible and fast (2.5 years vs. 7.5); trust = social achievement, not technical one
- **UNIVAC on CBS 1952** — predicted Eisenhower landslide; first mass public demonstration; trust built through spectacle
- **IBM "nobody got fired"** — trust reduced decision-making risk; the brand was the product as much as the machine
- **ENIAC operators** — women programmers (Jean Jennings, Frances Bilas, etc.) built the trust infrastructure for ENIAC but were erased from the official history; trust and prejudice are connected

## THE 5 CONCEPTS — ONE LINE EACH

| Concept | One line | The story to tell |
|---|---|---|
| **Agendas** | Histories are told to serve present interests — ask who benefits | Aiken→Babbage; McCorduck; "two boys in a garage" myth |
| **Appropriation** | Technology repurposed for unintended uses — value emerges in use, not design | Ferranti→Nim; SAGE→SABRE; GPU→AlexNet |
| **Defining technology** | Reshapes self-understanding, not just behaviour — provides a new cultural vocabulary | Cybernetics 1960s; Generative AI 2020s |
| **Surveillance capitalism** | Behaviour as raw material; prediction products; user is the product | Google AdWords; Netflix; Facebook |
| **Trust** | Technical superiority is never enough — social trust must be built | Hollerith; UNIVAC CBS; IBM brand |

## THE ONE THING BECKERS ALWAYS REWARDS

End every answer with this structure:

> *"[Restate thesis]. The deeper point is that [technology / narrative] is never neutral — it serves [specific actor] by [specific function]. This tension remains unresolved today in [one contemporary example]."*

That last sentence — the forward-pointing open question — is what separates a 1.5 from a 2.0.
