### What the model answer actually covers

Prof. Beckers included the full model answer. Key points:

1. **DEC PDP-series** → first *affordable* personal computers → "broadened appeal" by making technology accessible beyond big corporations (this IS 1980s relevant as a starting context)

5. **Entertainment / gaming** — *BUT* games were in a *lower ebb* in the 1980s — Pong/Space Invaders are early 70s/late 70s; don't use games as a 1980s broadening-appeal argument
6. The *real* 1980s broadening: **CD-ROMs, networks, productivity software** (word processors, spreadsheets like Lotus 1-2-3), making computers useful for ordinary people

## The 5 Rules for This Exam

### Rule 1: Answer the exact question, not adjacent history
The question says "1980s" — stay in the 1980s. If you mention 1960s events, *explicitly explain why they are relevant to the 1980s argument*.

### Rule 2: "Broadened the appeal" = accessibility to non-experts
This phrase has a specific meaning in Campbell-Kelly. It means: more people (non-programmers, non-corporations) could use, afford, and want computers. Every point in your essay must connect back to this.

### Rule 3: Name the firm AND say what they did AND why it broadened appeal
Wrong: "DEC produced computers."
Right: "DEC's PDP-series produced cheaper personal computers, which made the technology accessible to smaller organisations and eventually individuals, thus broadening the appeal beyond mainframe customers."

### Rule 4: Don't confuse chronology
- IBM unbundling: **1968** (not 1980s)
- Microsoft founding: **1975** (not a result of 1968 unbundling)
- DEC PDP: **1960s–70s** popular (not 1980s)
- Apple Macintosh GUI: **1984** ← this IS 1980s
- IBM PC: **1981** ← this IS 1980s, but focus on why it broadened appeal

### Rule 5: Depth > breadth
One well-developed point (firm + action + mechanism + effect on accessibility) beats five surface-level mentions. The model answer goes deep on each point.

## B-type Questions (Campbell-Kelly chapters) — General Tips

- B-questions ask you to *explain* or *discuss* a claim from the reading. The answer is in the book.
- Structure: (1) restate the claim precisely, (2) give 2–3 mechanisms/examples from the text, (3) name specific firms/people/dates
- Avoid: vague generalisations, modern analogies, going outside the timeframe
- Always use Campbell-Kelly's own framing ("broadened the appeal," "software crisis," "the computer utility") — this signals to the examiner you know the text

---

## C-type Concepts — General Tips

- These are short definition + significance questions
- Format: define the concept (1 sentence) + explain its historical significance (2–3 sentences) + optionally give one example
- Don't write a narrative — be direct and precise
